Overview

Bringing a stylized world to life

Harmony in the Wild is a 3D casual adventure game built in Unity using the Universal Render Pipeline.

Players control a fox exploring an island while working to restore the balance of nature and uncover what happened to their family.

My work has focused primarily on player-facing ability effects, environmental VFX, shaders and supporting visual systems.

Ability VFX

Snowdive, Charge Jump, Echo, ability acquisition and speed boosts.

Environmental VFX

Snowfall, butterflies, falling leaves, fireflies and other ambient systems.

Shader Work

Growth, rainbow, bioluminescence and other stylized rendering experiments.

Technical Integration

Integrating visual effects into gameplay and environmental systems.

Ability VFX

Giving player abilities a visual identity

A significant part of my work on Harmony has focused on visual effects connected directly to player abilities.

These include Snowdive, Charge Jump, Echo, ability acquisition and temporary speed boosts.

The effects range from compact gameplay feedback to larger moments built with Unity's VFX Graph.

Environmental VFX

Adding movement and atmosphere to the island

Environmental effects help give individual regions their own atmosphere and keep the world from feeling visually static.

One of my first systems was a snowfall effect for the arctic region, using VFX Graph with a 4×4 snowflake sprite sheet, turbulence and gravity.

VFX Graph made it possible to support a substantial particle count across a large environment while keeping the effect practical for real-time use.

Shader Work

Supporting the game's stylized visual language

Shader Graph work allowed me to extend Harmony's visual language beyond particle-driven effects.

This included experiments and production work around rainbow rendering, growth effects, bioluminescence and other stylized treatments.

These systems were designed to complement the game's soft, colorful presentation while still functioning within a real-time Unity workflow.

Supplemental Effects

Small systems that make the world feel responsive

Not every useful effect needs to be a major gameplay spectacle.

I also worked on smaller systems including character water-exit effects, bioluminescence, mesh-based jumping fish and other environmental feedback.

These details help connect the character to the environment and add movement to otherwise static spaces.
